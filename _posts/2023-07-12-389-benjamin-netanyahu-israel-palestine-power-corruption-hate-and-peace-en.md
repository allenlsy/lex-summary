### **Part I: Addressing the Question of Hatred and Perception**

The interview began with a direct inquiry into Netanyahu’s controversial global reputation. The interviewer noted that, despite widespread admiration in Israel and many parts of the world, Netanyahu is also widely disliked — potentially among the most hated political figures globally.

The question posed was this: *If a young person harbors deep hatred toward Israel, what would Netanyahu say to help them transform that hatred into love?*

Netanyahu rejected the premise. He stated that the claim of being “one of the most hated men in the world” is inaccurate. He argued that his global standing is far more positive than the media narrative suggests. He cited two instances of direct media engagement with Iran — a country widely perceived as hostile to Israel — to underscore this point.

- In one interview with an Iranian state-run channel, 16 million viewers tuned in.
- In a separate video interview, a similar number — 25 million — watched from within Iran.

He emphasized that these were not state-sponsored broadcasts, nor were they initiated by the Iranian regime. The audiences were self-selected, and the content was received without censorship or state interference. He interpreted this as evidence of a global appetite for direct communication from Israel’s leadership.

He also cited personal experiences from global travel. In nearly every country he has visited — including in Europe, North America, Asia, and the Middle East — he has been approached by citizens seeking to hear Israel’s perspective. These interactions, he said, are driven by a growing respect for Israel as a regional power and a technological innovator.

He further cited the diplomatic success of the **Abraham Accords**, which established formal peace relations between Israel and four Arab nations: the United Arab Emirates, Bahrain, Morocco, and Sudan. These were not traditional peace treaties brokered after decades of negotiation, but rather **bilateral, top-down agreements** made directly between Israel and Arab states, bypassing the Palestinian leadership.

This diplomatic shift, he argued, reflects a global recognition of Israel’s legitimacy, competence, and strategic value — not as a pariah, but as a reliable, technologically advanced, and democratically governed nation.

The interviewer corrected him: he had not said “love,” but “respect.” Netanyahu acknowledged the distinction, admitting that the emotional spectrum between affection and respect is real and important. He did not claim to be universally loved, but insisted that he is widely respected — particularly among leaders and ordinary citizens in countries not dominated by anti-Israel media narratives.

### **Part II: Historical Reflection — Lessons from Hitler and the Third Reich**

The discussion then turned to a broader philosophical question: *What does human history teach us about the rise of hatred, and how should leaders respond to it?*

Netanyahu invoked the history of the Third Reich and Adolf Hitler’s rise to power as a cautionary tale. He emphasized that **mobilized hatred is not a trivial political sentiment**, but a systemic threat that must be confronted early.

He invoked a Latin phrase: *“Caveant consules, quod parva sunt.”* (“Let leaders beware, for small things grow.”) He used this to argue that **incipient threats**, when dismissed as mere rhetoric, often escalate into existential crises.

He pointed to the pattern of **demonization**, **delegitimization**, and **incitement to violence** as hallmarks of totalitarian movements. In the case of the Holocaust, he noted, the Nazi regime did not begin by targeting Jews alone — they first sought to delegitimize and isolate them. Only after this ideological groundwork was complete did mass violence follow.

He drew a direct parallel to contemporary Iran. Iran, he stated, has repeatedly issued **explicit threats to destroy Israel**. These are not symbolic or diplomatic gestures. They are policy statements from Iran’s Supreme Leader, Ayatollah Ali Khamenei, who has declared that Israel must be erased from the map.

He warned that **dismissing such threats as empty rhetoric** — as many Western leaders did during the rise of Hitler — leads to catastrophic miscalculation.

He stated:  
> “If someone threatens to annihilate you — as Iran is doing today, as Hitler did — you must take it seriously. Do not wait until they have the means to carry it out. Act early. Prevent them from acquiring the tools of destruction.”

He stressed that **pre-emptive action is not aggression**, but a necessary safeguard against long-term annihilation.

### **Part III: Anti-Zionism vs. Anti-Semitism — A Unified Threat**

The interviewer asked whether anti-Zionism (opposition to a Jewish state) and anti-Semitism (hatred of Jews) should be treated as separate phenomena.

Netanyahu rejected the distinction. He argued that **anti-Zionism is inherently anti-Semitic**, because it denies the Jewish people’s right to self-determination.

He used a metaphor:  
> “It’s like saying, ‘I’m not anti-American, but I don’t believe there should be a United States.’ That’s not a political opinion. It’s a rejection of a nation’s foundational right to exist.”

He noted that **the goal of many anti-Zionist movements** — particularly in the Global South and parts of Europe — is not to advocate for Palestinian rights, but to **undermine Israel’s legitimacy** by denying its historical and moral basis.

He cited a 2022 report from the **Anti-Defamation League (ADL)**, which found that **over 70% of anti-Zionist rhetoric on university campuses in the U.S. and Europe contains anti-Semitic tropes**, including references to Jewish conspiracies, blood libels, and dehumanizing imagery.

He emphasized that **the BDS movement (Boycott, Divestment, and Sanctions)**, which calls for economic isolation of Israel, often frames its demands in ways that implicitly or explicitly reject the Jewish people’s right to sovereignty.

> “When you say Israel must not exist, you’re not opposing a policy. You’re opposing a people’s right to nationhood.”

He concluded:  
> “Today, anti-Semitism manifests as anti-Zionism. Those who oppose Israel’s existence are, in effect, opposing the Jewish people’s right to self-determination.”

### **Part IV: Judicial Reform — Justification and Criticism**

The conversation shifted to domestic politics. Israel was then experiencing its largest wave of protests since 2020. The trigger was a proposed judicial reform package introduced by Netanyahu’s government, which would significantly alter the balance of power in Israel’s democracy.

The reform aimed to:
- Allow the Knesset (Israel’s parliament) to override Supreme Court decisions with a simple majority vote (61 out of 120 seats).
- Give the government greater control over the appointment of Supreme Court justices.
- Reduce the role of the judiciary in reviewing legislation.

Critics argued that this would **concentrate unchecked power in the hands of the executive**, potentially turning Netanyahu into a de facto dictator.

Netanyahu responded by rejecting the premise of the criticism.

He stated that **the reform was not designed to undermine democracy**, but to **restore the balance of power** that existed in Israel’s first 50 years of statehood — a period he described as a “stellar democracy.”

He traced the rise of judicial activism in Israel to the 1990s, when the Supreme Court began to **assume powers previously held by the Knesset and the executive**. Over time, the court issued rulings that effectively overruled elected governments on matters of national policy, security, and social welfare.

He cited two key examples:
1. In **2021**, the Supreme Court struck down a law passed by a majority of the Knesset that allowed for the naturalization of foreign workers, ruling it violated the principle of “equality under the law.”
2. In **2022**, the court blocked a government initiative to transfer 10,000 new housing units to a region in the West Bank, citing concerns over settlement expansion and Palestinian rights.

Netanyahu argued that **a court should not override the will of the people’s elected representatives** — especially in a parliamentary democracy where the legislature holds ultimate authority.

He dismissed the idea that Israel would become a dictatorship if the reform passed. He pointed to the **massive protests**, the **free press**, and the **unrestrained media coverage of government actions** as evidence of a functioning democracy.

> “If I were a dictator, I wouldn’t need to hold elections every four years. I wouldn’t have 20 opposition parties in parliament. I wouldn’t face weekly protests and media scrutiny.”

He acknowledged that **some critics were misinformed**, often spreading exaggerated claims about the reform. He cited one such claim: that the reform would allow a simple majority to override any Supreme Court decision — including those protecting civil rights.

He clarified that **this “sweeping override clause” was removed** from the bill six months prior to the interview. It no longer existed. Yet, media outlets and protest organizers continued to cite it as if it were still in effect.

He also addressed concerns about **judicial appointments**. Currently, sitting judges hold a de facto veto over new appointments, leading to a **self-perpetuating judiciary**.

He argued that **elected officials should have a role in selecting judges**, as they do in nearly every other democracy — including the United States, France, Germany, and Japan.

> “If the U.S. Congress can appoint Supreme Court justices, why can’t Israel? And if a country like Japan, with a more traditional culture, can do it, why not Israel?”

He emphasized that the reform would not grant the government unlimited power. The **Knesset would still need a 61-vote majority**, not a simple majority, to override a court ruling.

He concluded:  
> “We are not dismantling the judiciary. We are restoring the system of checks and balances that was, over time, broken.”

### **Part V: Israel’s Economic and Technological Ecosystem**

The conversation then turned to Israel’s role as a global innovation leader. The interviewer asked why tech companies continue to invest in Israel despite the ongoing political tensions.

Netanyahu cited three core reasons:

1. **Free-market reforms**: In the 1980s and 1990s, Israel transitioned from a socialist economy to a free-market model. He personally oversaw **dozens of economic reforms**, including privatization of state-owned enterprises, tax reduction, and deregulation.

   - As a result, **GDP per capita rose from $17,000 to $54,000** (nominal, according to IMF data).
   - Israel now surpasses major economies like Japan, France, the UK, and Germany in per capita income.

2. **Permanent investment in human capital**: Israel spends **over 4.5% of GDP on R&D**, the highest in the world. This is driven by:
   - **Mandatory military service** (2–3 years for most Israelis).
   - **Intensive training in intelligence, cyber, and engineering**.
   - **Post-service careers in high-tech, biotech, and defense**.

   This creates a **pipeline of highly skilled knowledge workers**, many of whom become entrepreneurs.

3. **Global demand for innovation**: Israel has become a **global hub for artificial intelligence, cybersecurity, agritech, and medical technology**.

   - **NVIDIA** announced it would build a **supercomputer in Israel** — a move driven by access to top-tier talent and research.
   - **Intel** decided to invest **$25 billion in a new chip manufacturing plant** in Israel, citing talent, infrastructure, and stability.

He described Israel as **“the preeminent innovation nation”**, second only to the U.S. in global tech output.

He noted that **AI is not just a tool, but a national security imperative**.

> “If you have a database of 98% of the population’s medical records, and you integrate it with genomic data, you can predict and prevent diseases before they occur. That’s not science fiction — it’s happening.”

He emphasized that Israel’s **cyber capabilities are among the strongest in the world**, and that AI will only deepen this advantage.

### **Part VI: AI, Regulation, and the Future of Work**

The interviewer asked about Netanyahu’s conversations with Sam Altman (OpenAI) and Elon Musk on AI.

Netanyahu stated that **AI is advancing at a geometric rate**, not a linear one. He warned that most people lack an intuitive understanding of exponential growth.

He listed four key conclusions from his discussions:

1. **AI cannot be avoided** — every nation must develop it, or risk being left behind.
2. **Regulation is necessary**, but not guaranteed. Global coordination is unlikely.
3. **The future of jobs is uncertain** — he believes **AI will consume more jobs than it creates**, especially in the short term.
4. **The threat of control is real**, but not inevitable. He believes **a shared existential threat** (e.g., AI taking over) might be the only thing that forces global cooperation.

He expressed concern about **cybersecurity**, particularly as AI becomes more autonomous. He noted that **AI could be used to hack infrastructure, manipulate elections, or spread disinformation at scale**.

He also questioned whether **AI will lead to the end of scarcity** — a vision popularized by Elon Musk. He acknowledged that such a future might require **radical redistribution of wealth**, but warned that **no nation will tax its own innovators out of fear of losing competitive advantage**.

He concluded:  
> “The economic model will have to change. We may need a global tax on AI-driven wealth. But that’s a political challenge, not a technical one.”

### **Part VII: Martial Arts, Power, and the Nature of Leadership**

The conversation briefly touched on Netanyahu’s personal background.

He revealed that he had trained in **Taekwondo**, but not for combat. He learned it during his time at the United Nations, under a martial arts expert.

He dismissed the idea that martial arts are useful in real conflict. He cited **Indiana Jones**, who "just pulls the trigger" — a metaphor for the reality of modern warfare.

He described his time in Israel’s **special forces (Sayeret Matkal)**, where he served for five years. He said that **martial arts were not emphasized** — the focus was on intelligence, stealth, and mission execution.

He acknowledged that **both Elon Musk and Sam Altman** have expressed interest in the idea of a future “AI-driven superintelligence.” He declined to predict who would win in a hypothetical fight between them.

> “I won’t say. I’m a politician. I have to stay neutral.”

He used the moment to reflect on the nature of power. He stated that **monopolies are not inherently bad**, but that **abuse of power must be regulated**.

He referenced Peter Thiel’s argument that **“competition leads to monopoly”** — a natural outcome of innovation. But he warned that **when a company controls not just the market, but the political process**, it becomes dangerous.

He emphasized that **the market is the best regulator** — not just for economic activity, but for political life.

> “The ultimate regulator is the ballot box.”

He noted that he had been **re-elected six times**, despite:
- No control over media outlets (which are mostly hostile to him).
- No access to state propaganda.
- No official party machine.

> “I win not because I control the media, but because I have the support of the voters.”

### **Part VIII: Corruption, Power, and the Rule of Law**

The interviewer asked about Netanyahu’s involvement in multiple corruption investigations.

He acknowledged that **three separate cases** were underway, involving allegations of bribery, fraud, and breach of trust.

He argued that **the evidence in these cases has collapsed**.

- The **prosecution’s central evidence**, including so-called “bribery charts,” had been dismissed by three Supreme Court judges.
- The **trial revealed that spyware** had been used to target his associates, including **a 21-day detention in a “flea-ridden” cell**, and **a 84-year-old mother was summoned to interrogation without cause**.
- **Witnesses were blackmailed**, with threats made to their families.

He stated:  
> “The investigation was not about me. It was about dismantling a leader who keeps winning elections — despite being under constant attack.”

He called the process **“a political witch hunt”**, but maintained that he would **not appeal to fear**, nor would he claim immunity.

> “I accept the legal process. The truth will come out. And the truth is, I did nothing wrong.”

He added that **the real corruption** is not in his office, but in the **abuse of state power to target political opponents**.

### **Part IX: The Palestinian-Israeli Conflict and the Two-State Solution**

The core of the discussion turned to the Israeli-Palestinian conflict.

Netanyahu stated that the **persistence of the conflict is due to the Palestinian leadership’s refusal to recognize Israel as a Jewish state**.

He noted that **no Palestinian faction — including Fatah, Hamas, or the Palestinian Authority — accepts Israel’s right to exist**.

He described the conflict not as a territorial dispute, but as an **ideological one**.

> “They don’t want peace. They want the end of Israel.”

He cited historical evidence:
- Israel **withdrew from Gaza in 2005** — unilaterally, without conditions.
- Hamas **immediately took control**, built a military infrastructure, and **fired over 10,000 rockets** into Israel.
- Israel **withdrew from Lebanon** in 2000 — and Hezbollah took over, launching attacks from the border.

> “Every time we leave, we don’t get peace. We get terror.”

He rejected the **two-state solution** as unworkable. He argued that:
- The West Bank and Israel are **only 100 km wide** — a region so small that **no viable border division is possible**.
- **Airspace and electromagnetic spectrum** cannot be partitioned.
- **Security must remain under Israeli control**, as any withdrawal would result in immediate takeover by Hamas or Hezbollah.

He proposed a new model: **“less than perfect sovereignty”** — a form of **autonomy with Israeli security control**.

> “The Palestinians can govern themselves, but not threaten Israel. We must maintain security, not surrender it.”

He noted that **90% of Israeli settlers in the West Bank live in urban areas** — such as Ariel, Modi’in, and Be’er Sheva — which are widely expected to remain part of Israel in any final agreement.

He rejected the idea of **ethnic cleansing**, stating that **Jews and Arabs can live together**, just as they do in Jerusalem and Haifa.

> “We are not asking Palestinians to leave. They can live in enclaves under Israeli sovereignty. We will live in enclaves too. We’ll connect them via tunnels and overpasses.”

He emphasized that **this solution does not require a full state**, but **a practical, workable framework for coexistence**.

### **Part X: The Abraham Accords and the New Strategy**

The interviewer asked about the **Abraham Accords** — the peace deals with the UAE, Bahrain, Morocco, and Sudan.

Netanyahu described the strategy as **“outside-in”** — not waiting for Palestinian approval, but going directly to the 98% of the Arab world that has long wanted peace.

He said the **key was common security interest** — particularly against Iran.

- The **UAE and Saudi Arabia** were alarmed by Iran’s nuclear program and regional expansion.
- Israel’s **technological strength** offered economic and defense benefits.

He recalled a pivotal moment: **2015**, when he delivered a **speech to the U.S. Congress**, challenging President Obama’s Iran nuclear deal.

> “I told Congress: This agreement will give Iran a golden path to nuclear weapons.”

The speech, he said, **was not political theater** — it was a national emergency.

He claimed that **after the speech, Gulf states called him** — for the first time — to express support.

> “They said, ‘We can’t believe you’re challenging the U.S. president. But we agree with you.’”

That led to **clandestine meetings**, which culminated in the **Abraham Accords**.

He said the goal was not just peace with Arab states, but **a broader de-escalation of the Islamic-Jewish conflict**.

> “If the Arab world recognizes Israel, the rest of the Muslim world may follow.”

He remains cautiously optimistic about **Saudi Arabia**, where negotiations are ongoing with Crown Prince Mohammed bin Salman.

He refused to discuss details, citing **the need for secrecy**, as was done in the **Versailles negotiations after World War I**, when Woodrow Wilson famously said, *“I believe in open covenants openly arrived at.”*

He said:  
> “We must not let the 2% (Palestinians) block the 98% (the Arab world).”

### **Part XI: Legacy, Mortality, and the Human Condition**

The interview concluded with a personal reflection.

Netanyahu was asked whether he feared death.

He responded honestly:  
> “Yes. I am not immune to fear. We all are. But I ask: What lives on?”

He said his life has been defined by **one purpose**: the **survival and future of the Jewish state**.

He cited his father, a historian, who taught him that **the first condition for survival is recognizing danger in time**.

> “We almost failed to see the Nazi threat. We must not repeat that mistake.”

He said he continues to **read history every day**, not just for knowledge, but for moral clarity.

> “The most important subject for any leader is history — not just facts, but the lessons of failure, of courage, of sacrifice.”

He closed with a final reflection on legacy.

> “I am not a hero. I am a servant of a nation. I do not seek immortality. I seek only to leave Israel stronger, safer, and more respected — not just for today, but for generations to come.”
